Which debris should we
take responsibility for first?

Six real objects circle above us, drawn from the dataset this challenge provides. This demo walks through a suggested framework for deciding which of them humanity should take responsibility for first: it asks seven questions — grounded in Mitákuye Oyás'iŋ, Škaŋ, our relationship with the stars, and a duty to the seventh generation — then shows what your answers would mean. It is a starting point for deliberation, offered openly — a framework meant to be questioned, adapted and improved over time.

          What this tool cannot decide

          • Whether Lakota principles can be converted into numbers. They shaped the design; they are not variables in it.
          • Whether an intervention is lawful. That belongs to operators, treaties and the communities affected.
          • Whether the data is complete. Six objects stand in for thousands; every dimension score is a deliberation starter, not a measurement.
          • Whether acting is wise. It can only make the consequences of acting — and of waiting — visible.
          • It cannot yet hear the communities who will live with this decision — the multilingual, multicultural, multinational public under a shared sky. That is the work of the expanded framework.

          The tool ends where human deliberation begins. Its output is a conversation to have, not a decision made.

          If a decision protects orbit today but leaves a worse environment to those who come after — was it a responsible decision?

          This tool does not translate Lakota knowledge into an algorithm. The principles named here — Mitákuye Oyás'iŋ, Škaŋ, relationship with the stars, Seven Generations — were shared in the Challenge 2 materials as starting points for co-design; they shaped what the tool asks and what it refuses to ignore. Lakota knowledge holders retain authority over how these principles are represented, expressed and used. The framework is documented in Seven Generations in Orbit: A Framework for Responsible Debris Prioritization (S. Al-Madi, Linguistic Security Institute, 2026), included in this repository.

          An instrument for deliberation — not a final judgement.

          It opens the conversation about which pieces of debris humanity should take responsibility for first. It does not close it. Every ranking it shows is a starting point for the people who must decide together.
